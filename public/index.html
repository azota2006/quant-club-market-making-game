    How to play

    1 · What this is

    You're a market maker quoting live prices on a handful of commodities. Each commodity's true value is secretly determined by a shuffled deck of cards. You are never trading anyone's actual cards — you're trading a number, like a futures contract that pays out whatever the true value turns out to be.

    2 · Where the number comes from

    Each commodity is a suit. Its true value is the sum of the ranks (A=1, 2–10 face value, J=11, Q=12, K=13) of only the cards of that suit that actually got dealt into someone's hand. Cards left over after dealing are discarded and count toward nothing. Nobody — not even the host — knows this number until Reveal.

    3 · Your edge

    You only ever see your own hand. That's a guaranteed floor for any commodity you hold cards in. Combine it with the public game numbers on your screen (decks in play, active commodities, cards per player, players in the round) to estimate the rest: every card in a suit averages rank 7, so you can put a number on the cards you can't see.

    That gives you a personal fair value — different from everyone else's, because everyone's hand is different. That difference is what makes a market worth making.

    4 · How trading works

    For each commodity you post a bid (what you'll pay) and an ask (what you'll sell for). Either side can be left blank. Anyone can Lift your ask or Hit your bid at any time, and your quote stays live until you change or clear it — so a bad price can be hit again and again.

    Every trade is exactly 1 unit; click repeatedly to build size. A trade moves cash and position between the two of you. It never moves cards.

    5 · How you win

    At Reveal, every commodity's true value is shown. Your round P&L is:

    (cash now − starting cash)  +  Σ (your position × true value)

    Highest total across all rounds wins the night.

    6 · Worked example

    1. 2 decks, 4 suits, 16 players → a 104-card deck, 6 cards each.
    2. 96 cards get dealt; 8 are discarded and gone for good.
    3. You're dealt 2 Hearts summing to 9.
    4. Hearts should be about a quarter of the 96 dealt cards → roughly 24 Hearts out there, so about 22 you can't see.
    5. 22 unseen cards × average rank 7 ≈ 154.
    6. Your personal fair value for Hearts ≈ 9 + 154 = 163. You quote 158 / 168.
    7. Someone lifts your 168 → your cash +168, your Hearts position −1. Theirs: cash −168, position +1.
    8. Hearts reveals at 172. They were long into a higher value: +4. You were short: −4. No cards ever changed hands.
